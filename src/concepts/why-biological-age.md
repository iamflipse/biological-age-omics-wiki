# Why Use Biological Age as the Endpoint

The paper places biological age either as **the main outcome** of a study or as **the middle link** (mediator) between lifestyle and function. It gives three reasons:

1. **Better prediction.** Many measures predict disability, frailty, multimorbidity and all-cause death better than calendar age alone. That turns observational findings into usable risk sorting.
2. **Sensitive to lifestyle.** They pick up the cumulative effect of environment and behaviour, so researchers can ask how activity, sleep and diet relate to *whole-body* ageing rather than one disease at a time.
3. **Re-measurable.** You can test someone repeatedly and model the within-person change, which gives stronger evidence about what comes first and whether an intervention works.

The organizing chain the paper proposes:

**Lifestyle → multi-omics changes → biological age → function, illness burden, survival**
