The Big Picture

In one line: your biological age — how old your body acts, measured from blood, DNA marks, proteins, metabolites or gut bacteria — is a better target for healthy ageing than your birthday, and the three habits with the most evidence for moving it are physical activity, sleep and diet.

Figure 1 from the paper (CC BY 4.0).

The paper's argument in five steps

  1. Medicine is shifting from treating single diseases to protecting function and resilience. People the same age differ hugely in decline, illness and survival, and calendar age can't explain why. → What Is Biological Age?
  2. Biological age can now be measured with several kinds of "clocks": blood-test clocks, DNA-methylation clocks, protein and metabolite clocks, and gut-microbiome clocks. → The Four Kinds of Clocks
  3. It makes a better endpoint because it predicts disability, frailty, multimorbidity and death better than calendar age, reacts to lifestyle, and can be re-measured to track change. → Why Use Biological Age
  4. Research exploded after 2017 and again after 2023, and the biggest lifestyle themes are physical activity, sleep and diet. → Research Map
  5. Each habit links to a younger biological age, probably through inflammation, metabolism and oxidative stress. The three work together. → Activity · Sleep · Diet · Together

What the paper does not claim

  • It doesn't present new trial results. It's a review plus a map of the literature.
  • It says no single clock captures whole-body ageing.
  • It says the causal evidence for sleep and activity is still evolving. Most findings are associations.
